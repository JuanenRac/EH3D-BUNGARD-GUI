# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented here.

## [Unreleased]

- **A bit of the catalogue takes the material of the board into the tool:** besides the limits of the spindle, the turns and the feed of the tool are multiplied by the factors of the material of the board of the job (the same as the default values: PTFE, ceramic and aluminium ask for less), and the details of the bit show the material applied, its factors, the depth and the passes. A note says when the material asks for fewer turns than the bit or than the spindle can give. The depth is checked: a router gets its passes from the board plus the extra and the bite of the bit (and its depth is raised to that when it was less), and a bit whose cutting length does not go through the board and the extra is warned. The window of tools marks, next to the tool, a catalogue bit that is not suitable for the board. 11 new tests and the new texts in the 37 languages. Not tried on a machine.

- **A job left in the middle goes on with the whole job:** the file that is kept while a job runs now also lists the programs that wait their turn (the routing after the drilling) and the tools that are switched off, so after a stop the rest of the drilling goes on, then the routing, and the tools that were off stay off (before, only the program that was running came back). 2 tests.

## [0.1.1] - No fixed English left in the window and every label checked in 37 languages

- **No fixed English words left in the window:** the labels of the bar of the job (job, Z feed, spindle, tool), the tag of the modern interface, the column of the series of the catalogue, the kinds of link (demo, node, server), the maker "Generic" and the names of the materials that are words (epoxy, bare copper, paper, sheets, series, aluminium, flexible) now come from the texts of the interface in the 37 languages. The notes of the materials stay in English for now. 1 test keeps words of the interface out of the code that paints the window.
- **The widths are checked in every language:** a test draws the launcher, the viewer and every window in the 37 languages and fails when the text of a button, a switch or a list does not fit its room even made smaller; the text of a button is now made smaller (and cut with an ellipsis as a last resort) like the one of a switch. The longest labels of the two switches of the plan view were shortened in Greek, Dutch, Russian and Ukrainian. 2 tests.

## [0.1.0] - 37 languages, several files at once, professional examples and a job that does the drilling and the routing

- **37 languages in the whole window:** Bulgarian, Croatian, Czech, Danish, Dutch, Estonian, Finnish, Greek, Hungarian, Irish, Latvian, Lithuanian, Maltese, Polish, Romanian, Slovak, Slovenian, Swedish, Norwegian, Icelandic, Ukrainian, Russian, Serbian, Albanian, Catalan, Galician, Basque, Brazilian Portuguese and Canadian French join the first eight; the list of languages scrolls and the text of every button and switch is drawn smaller, and cut with an ellipsis if it still does not fit, where a language needs more room. The new languages are translations by the model that wait for a review by native speakers; the nodes of the machine keep the first eight for now.

- **Turning the work keeps it where it is:** the work turns on its own middle, wherever it was moved to (before, it turned on the corner of the file it started from), and a turned work that would leave the table is brought back inside it; a board that stayed where it was (moving the design alone) turns on its own middle too. 1 test.

- **Several files at once:** the Open window chooses two or more files with Ctrl+click or Shift+click and opens them together, drilling and routing at the same time. Several files of the same kind are joined into one job (the holes of all of them, or the paths of all of them); their tools are numbered by diameter so that two files that number theirs differently do not clash. The files chosen, not the joined one, are the recent ones. 3 tests.
- **Four professional examples** in the `examples` folder, each with its drilling file (with the diameters of its tools declared) and its routing file: a 32-bit microcontroller board, a four-channel analog front end with an isolation slot, a 2.4 GHz amplifier and filter with via fences and isolation milling along the 50 ohm line, and a 5 V 3 A power supply. A test opens each and checks the tools, the diameters and that they fit the table.

## [0.0.9] - Drilling, routing or both, tool by tool

- **Drilling, routing or both:** the job has the drilling and the routing switched on or off (in the left panel, over the list of tools, and in the tools window), and both on run one after the other (the drilling first) without the machine of the 3D view going home in between; with only one on, only that one runs; with none on, the person is told. Before this only the drilling could be run.
- **Tools switched off:** the dot of each tool, in the left panel and in the tools window, leaves that tool (its holes or its paths) out of the job; it is shown as a ring and its text goes dim. 3 tests.

## [0.0.8] - Two spindles, the catalogue into the tools and the machine of the 3D view

- **Two spindles, chosen in the settings (Machine tab):** the original one (60000 rpm) and the one with its variable-frequency drive (24000 rpm). Each has its limits (slowest and fastest turns, power, the fastest XY and Z feed the machine holds with it, the thickest bit), kept in the settings file with a line of their own so that they can be tuned.
- **Take a bit of the catalogue into a tool:** every tool of the window of tools has a Catalogue button; the bit picked there gives its turns, feeds and passes to that tool (the plunge feed and turns for a drill; passes, XY and Z feeds and turns for a router). The catalogue and the window of tools show the spindle in use.
- **Warnings by spindle:** where the spindle cannot give what a bit asks for, the catalogue says so and suggests the values that it can use: the same bite for each turn when it turns slower, and slower XY or Z when the machine cannot hold the maker's feed. A bit that is thicker than the spindle takes, or that asks for far more turns than it has, is marked as not compatible and cannot be taken into a tool. 11 tests.
- **The list of boards shows 16 materials at once** (it showed 9), so a maker with twenty references needs a single scroll.
- **Choosing a sound or a voice no longer plays it:** it is heard only with the Test button.
- **The machine of the 3D view is a machine:** it has a place of its own (home) and a start like the real one. At rest the head waits at home, and moving the board or the design in the plan view, with a board or without it, never moves the machine (it used to follow the first hole). When a job starts the machine powers up, goes home, travels to the first point of the work and only then does the job begin, with the head following it, for drilling and for routing; when the job ends or is stopped the head goes home again. The speed of the simulation scales all of it. 11 tests.
- **Arrows on the bars of the lists:** the scroll bar of every list (the drop-down lists, the tools, the catalogue, the browser of files) has an arrow at each end that moves the list one row.
- **The Head node button** of the Connection window is in the bottom row, with Test, Save and Close, instead of over the row of the kinds of connection.

## [0.0.7] - Light and quality check with the camera of the drill head

- **Sounds for the visual check:** two more sounds in the settings (the Job tab), for a check of the board that is correct and for one that is not, chosen like the other two: off, the beep of the program (a rise for correct, three low falling notes for not correct), one of the sixty of the catalogue, a file of yours or a voice that says "The visual check of the board is correct" or "... is not correct" (`check_ok.wav` and `check_bad.wav` in every voice of the folder `voices`, in English, Spanish, Italian, German, French, Portuguese and Chinese). "Cannot judge" has no sound of its own, because it is neither. They are off until chosen; a voice without the recording plays the beep. 1 test.

- **Light and quality check with the camera of the drill head:** the window of the node has the ring of LEDs (off and three levels, shown as the node says it) and a button for the new Quality check window. That window shows the camera with a mark on every hole it looked at, a verdict as big as a sign (OK, NOT OK or cannot judge), how many holes are right, the width of the milled channels and a line for each finding; it chooses the checks (holes, channels), the profile (standard, fine pitch, prototype), the light for the picture, the hole of the job that is in the centre (the holes of the job around it that the camera sees are judged too), the scale of the picture and its calibration from the hole just measured. A second window holds the tolerances. The profile is kept in a file next to the settings and, through the server, given to it. The ring says the result in colour. 8 tests; `--check` and `--hole N` for pictures.

## [0.0.6] - Scrolling lists, a catalogue of more than 450 bits, tabs and colours by tool, and subtle animations

- **Quiet animations:** the screens and the windows fade in as they open (the windows also rise a few points); while a job runs, the ring of the head breathes and sends out a ripple, the dashes of the paths still to cut move along them, a light crosses the progress bar and the tool that is working breathes in the list. Nothing moves when no job runs and nothing is drawn while a picture is taken; the window redraws 15 times a second at rest and 60 while something moves.
- **Three decorative sample boards:** `06_sun_badge`, `06_owl` and `06_heart` (a drill file and a routing file each) have rings and meshes of holes of several sizes, a shaped outline, windows and the isolation of tracks with a V-bit, to try the views, the tools, the panelization and the tabs on something better than a rectangle.
- **The library of base materials has the sizes the makers publish:** the Fortex boards in their seven sizes of 1.6 mm, the MG Chemicals 500 series in inches, the Rogers and Taconic panel sizes from their data sheets, the master sheets of the glass-epoxy laminates, and 0.2 mm in the photosensitive boards of Bungard.
- **A catalogue of more than 450 bits, from the maker's tables:** the drills of the micro series (from 0.05 mm) and the small, medium and large series up to 7 mm, the slot drills, and the diamond-cut, down-cut, up-cut, chip-breaker and diamond-coated routers of a PCB tool maker, all with the 1/8 inch shank, their series, the length of the flute and the turns, the feeds and the depth of a pass for FR-4 as the maker's tables give them, next to the generic end mills, V-bits, ball noses, spot drills and countersinks (twelve kinds in all).
- **A bit is drawn when it is clicked:** the window shows its shape (the flutes, the point, the diamond cut, the chip breaker, the V of the tip) with the diameter, the length of the flute, the shank and the total length, and says in a few words what the kind is for, in the eight languages.
- **The spindle sets the speeds:** a setting of the catalogue window (24 000 turns by default, kept between runs) is the top speed of the spindle; a bit whose table speed is higher is shown at that speed with its feeds lowered in the same proportion, so the bite of each turn stays as the maker wants it.
- **The list of tools scrolls:** the tools of the job card no longer push the layers out of the window; the rows that fit between the file name and the layers are shown, the rest are reached with a bar at the side of the list or the mouse wheel, and a smaller window simply shows fewer rows.
- **The drop-down lists scroll:** the vertical bar of every open list (the maker, the thickness, the sounds, the language, the files of the opener) can be dragged, clicked and moved with the wheel; before, it only drew.
- **Move only the design:** a button of the 2D view lets the design (the holes and the paths) be dragged over the board and the table, which stay where they are.
- **Each routing tool in its own colour:** the paths of the 2D and 3D views take the colour of the tool that cuts them, the same as the swatch in the list of tools, so a file with three mills shows three colours instead of one.
- **Tabs on a single board, and only on its outside:** the tabs of the Panelization window are cut into the outside of a board even when there is one copy, and only into the outside (the closed path that holds the others); the pockets, slots and tracks inside are routed whole. The views show the gaps and the job routes with the lifts.
- **A second routing file for the sample board:** `05_board_tracks.plt` has the isolation of the tracks as a plot of its own, to open beside the outline.

## [0.0.5] - The catalogue of bits, tracks on the sample board, spoken notices, the drill head node window and jobs that follow the table

- **Spoken notices:** the end of the job and the request for a tool can be said by a voice in eight languages (`sound_finish = voice:LANG:f|m`, chosen from the list of sounds); the folder `voices/` has synthetic voices (Piper, free to give away) for English, Spanish, Italian, German, French, Portuguese and Chinese, and the person's own WAV files replace them; the program tells which voices are there.
- **The node of the drill head:** a window (from the Connection window) sets how EH3D-NODE-BUNGARD-AUX is reached (not used, directly or through the server, which relays it with the address, the token and the machine id of the connection window), its address and token and whether its camera is shown; it shows the picture of the camera, the speed and the temperature of the spindle, the protect output and a measure of the table under the head. Through the server it says the relay does not exist yet.
- **The jobs follow the table:** a check box of the calibration tab makes the drilling and the routing correct their Z with the map of the table (only a complete map, only when asked for).
- **Catalogue of bits:** a window (from the Tools window) lists 115 drills, end mills, down-cut and up-cut routers, diamond-cut routers, V-bits, ball noses, spot drills and countersinks, all with the 1/8 inch (3.175 mm) shank of the chuck of a Bungard machine, by kind, with the turns, the feeds and the depth of a pass to start from; a V-bit tells how wide its groove is at a depth.
- **A sample board with tracks:** the sample board has the isolation of four tracks, cut with a V-bit (tool 3 of the routing table).
## [0.0.4] - Drop-down lists, sixty sounds for each event, brighter job buttons, tabs for panels, table calibration and jobs that go on after a crash

- **Calibration of the table:** a new tab of the Settings window measures the height of the table in a grid of points (7 by 5 by default, up to 15 by 15), draws the map (blue low, red high) with the spread and the tilt in mm per metre, and keeps it with the machine; until a probe node is connected the probe is simulated.
- **The job goes on after a crash:** while a job runs, what it needs to go on (the file, the panel, the holes drilled) is saved in real time; if the window, the computer or the link goes down, the next start asks whether to go on from where it was or to cancel it. A job that ends or is stopped on purpose leaves nothing behind.
- **Brighter job buttons:** green to start, blue to pause, red to stop; the window opens maximized.
- **Panelization with tabs:** the outside of the panel can be routed with tabs; the person chooses how many on each side, how wide and where (spread, near the ends or together in the middle), and the plan view shows the cut paths.
- **More drop-down lists:** the maker, the thickness and the size of the board are chosen from lists instead of rows of buttons, and so is the place of the tabs.
- **Drop-down lists:** the theme, the language, the model of the machine and the sounds are chosen from a list that scrolls instead of rows of buttons; the end of the job and the request for a tool each have 60 sounds from the high ones to the low ones, the standard tone, none or a file of your own, and a sound that is chosen is played.
- **The first hole is drilled:** the simulation marked the hole the head was going to, one ahead of the one it had drilled, so the first one stayed open until the end; the window now asks the classic engine for the order of the holes and marks the right one.
- **Pictures on the buttons of the job:** Start, Pause and Stop and the emergency button show a play triangle, two bars, a square and a warning sign instead of words.
- **The tools window over a file you open:** when a file is opened from the Open window, the recent files or by dropping it, the window of the tools opens over it, as the classic programs show the tools of a file after loading it; the check box "Show the tools window when I open a file" in the settings turns it off (a file named on the command line does not open it).
- **Machines and stations window:** with the server connection, a new window lists every machine of the server grouped by station, what each one is doing, its job with the progress bar and the jobs ready, refreshes itself every few seconds without stopping the window, and chooses the machine the jobs are sent to; `--fleet FILE.json` shows a saved answer, `--modal fleet` opens it at the start.
- **The views follow the machine model:** choosing a model in the settings draws the 3D machine of that very model (not the one the table is most like) and starts the 2D and 3D views again for the new table.
- **Eleven themes:** Midnight, Paper (a light one), Forest, Retro terminal, Ocean and High contrast join the five themes; the settings window lists them all, each named in the language of the window, and the click on the theme in the title takes the next one.
## [0.0.3] - Gerber and DXF outlines as routing jobs, plotter arcs and circles, recent files, the count of holes of each drill and screenshots

- **Screenshots in the README:** six pictures of the window (the viewer, a job running, a second theme, the tools, the settings and the board) in all the eight languages of the README.
- **Recent files:** the Open window lists the last files opened (up to six, kept between runs) under the places, and a click opens one again.
- **DXF drawings:** the drawing of a board in a DXF file opens as a routing job, from the window, the command line and ZIP archives, with its lines, arcs, circles and polylines joined into paths.
- **The holes of each drill are counted:** the tools window shows, for every drill, the holes drilled in all the jobs (kept between runs, as the plan of the machine's own programs keeps its tool usage) and a button that starts the counts again; a job that ends or is stopped adds the holes it did.
- **Circles and arcs of plotter files:** a plotter file with `CI`, `AA`, `AR`, `SC` and `IP` is drawn and run as it is meant, cut into chords by the chord angle of the routing configuration.
- **Gerber outlines:** the profile layer of a board (`.gko`, `.gm1`, `.gml` or a `.gbr` that calls itself the profile or the edge) opens as a routing job, in the window, from the command line and from ZIP archives; a layer of copper is said not to be an outline instead of being cut.
- **A job that does not fit on the table is refused:** Start checks every hole and every routing point against the table and says so, in the language of the window, instead of finding it out on the machine.

## [0.0.2] - The Windows-style Open window, the board library, the tools with default and typed values, the connection and the settings windows, and buttons that answer to the pointer

- **The settings in tabs, with the machine models:** the General and Job tabs, every Bungard MTC and ATC model with its table, its tool changer and its limits, and the units of the steps and the coordinates (mm or mil), all with a real effect on the job.
- **An emergency button:** it can be shown or hidden, dragged to where it is wanted, and it stops the job at once.
- **Start and pause follow the job:** Start goes out when the job runs, only Pause and Stop answer, and Start comes back with either of them; the simulation goes on while another program is in front.
- **The 2D view follows the work:** a button centres on each drill or router, the drilling and the routing files turn together, the board can be moved on the table, and the view can be enlarged over the 3D one.
- **Living check boxes and text boxes:** they change with a soft animation under the pointer and when they are clicked.
- **Sounds:** a sound when the job ends and another at a tool change, each one chosen, tried and switched on or off in the settings.
- **A bigger library of boards:** more than a hundred base materials of many makers, with a list that scrolls.
- **The holes of each tool:** the tools window shows, next to the diameter of every drill, how many holes the board has for it.
- **The buttons answer to the pointer:** they light up with a glow and a stronger edge under it, sink and darken when they are pressed, have a shadow and a shine on their top edge, and the pointer turns into a hand over them.
- **The simulation goes at the speed of the machine:** a job takes the time it would take with the feed and the speed of each tool; the Settings window chooses how many times faster than the machine it runs (x0.5 to x50).
- **Every value can be typed:** the numbers with buttons to step them (the table, the panel, the board, the feeds and the speeds of the tools) can be clicked and written with the keyboard (a comma is a decimal point; Enter or a click elsewhere accepts, Esc leaves it as it was).
- **Default values and the operator's:** the tools have default values chosen from the diameter of the drill and the material of the board (per tool or all at once), and any value can be typed over them.
- **A lighter debug build:** the `dev` profile keeps the file and line of a panic (`debug = "line-tables-only"`) but not the full debug symbols, so the `target` folder of a test run is about a tenth of its size.
- **Open files:** an Open window with a folder browser (and the drives), files dropped on the window, Ctrl+O, and files given on the command line; a drilling file, a routing file or a ZIP archive is opened as what its extension says.
- **Move around the views:** the plan view is dragged and zoomed with the wheel, the 3D view is turned with a drag, zoomed with the wheel and moved with Shift or the right button; a button (or a double click) puts the view back.
- **Panelization:** copies of the board in columns and rows, placed by the gap between boards, by the distance from one to the next, or as many as fit in the table; the copies are drawn and the job makes them.
- **Settings and About:** the usable width and depth of the table can be changed and saved to the machine files; the About window says the version, the licence and the connection.
- **Connection and pause:** a Connection window chooses the link (the computer's RS-232, a USB-RS232 adapter, the EH3D node or the EH3D server), fills in the port, address, token or machine, tests it without blocking the window and saves it to the machine file without touching its other lines; Pause holds a running job between two moves; the 3D view also tilts up and down.
- **The Open window is made like the one of any Windows program:** the places on the left (the examples, the desktop, the documents, the downloads and the drives), the path above (it can be typed), Back and Up, the list with name, date, type and size that is sorted by a click on its heading, a click that chooses and a second one that opens, the file name box, the type of file (all job files, drilling, routing, ZIP, all) and Open and Cancel.
- **The board:** a library of base materials of many makers (Bungard, Fortex, CIF, generic FR-4, CEM-1, CEM-3 and FR-2, Isola, Panasonic, Shengyi, ITEQ, Ventec, Nelco, Arlon, Rogers, Taconic, Bergquist and DuPont) with their thickness, copper, standard formats, dielectric constant, loss and glass transition; the Board window chooses the material, the thickness, the copper, one or two copper sides and the size, and it is remembered. The nominal values are those of the makers' and sellers' lists: the exact ones are the datasheet's.
- **The tools:** the left card lists every tool of the job, marked drilling or routing, with its size, its feed and its speed, and lights the one that is working; the Tools window changes the extra stroke or the depth, the passes, the feeds and the speed of each tool and saves them to the tool files.
- **The settings window:** the theme and the language can be chosen there, whether the job asks at the tool changes and at the start of the spindle, and whether this window opens directly the next time, besides the usable size of the table.
- **A demo board that fits itself:** the drilling file and the routing file of the example are the same 100 x 80 mm board, with four drill sizes and two routing tools.
- **Command line:** files without an option are opened, and `--modal`, `--panel` and `--yaw` set the window, the panel and the turn of the 3D view for the pictures.
- **Repository:** the README in eight languages with the architecture and structure pictures, the CI and release workflows (the program is built for Windows 64 and 32 bits, Linux and macOS, with the test files as examples), the issue and pull-request templates and the contribution, security and support notes.

## [0.0.1] - The launcher and the viewer

- **Jobs in ZIP archives:** `--zip JOB.ZIP` opens a job with its drilling files (`.ncd`, `.drl`...) and routing files (`.plt`, `.hpgl`...) inside; the first of each is shown and the others are listed to be loaded with a click. Names that would leave the work folder are not listed, and no file is read past a size limit.
- **The launcher:** the modern interface or the classic drilling and routing programs for a machine with manual or automatic tool change; the choice can be remembered.
- **The viewer:** the plan view of the table with every hole by drill size and the routing paths over it, the isometric view of the machine fitted to the table size of the configuration, the live progress of the job and the questions of the machine to the operator.
- **Themes and languages:** Forge, Neon Arcade, Glass Pro, classic Windows and the colours of Windows; English, Spanish, Italian, German, French, Portuguese, Chinese and Japanese.
- **The machine:** driven by the engines of the classic programs with their machine file (the computer's RS-232, a USB-RS232 adapter, the EH3D node directly or through the EH3D server); tried on the simulator only.
